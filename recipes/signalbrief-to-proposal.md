# Recipe: SignalBrief to Proposal

Use this recipe when market intelligence needs to become a client-ready consulting, automation, product, or implementation proposal.

## Goal

Turn external signal into a clear business proposal with problem framing, opportunity, recommended scope, deliverables, timeline, and next steps.

## Inputs

- SignalBrief or equivalent research summary
- Target company, buyer, or market
- User's business objective
- Known budget or urgency
- Existing relationship/context
- Desired offer type: audit, discovery, MVP build, automation, consulting, retainer, or implementation

## Steps

### 1. Extract Decision Signals

Pull out the strongest evidence:

- recent company movement,
- hiring signals,
- customer complaints,
- competitor pressure,
- operational pain,
- product gaps,
- buyer language,
- urgency indicators.

### 2. Define the Business Problem

Write the problem in plain language:

- What is happening?
- Why does it matter now?
- What does the client lose if they ignore it?
- What decision does the proposal help them make?

### 3. Map Signal to Opportunity

Convert research into an offer:

| Signal | Implication | Offer Angle |
|---|---|---|
| Customer complaints | Existing workflow is painful | Automation or UX cleanup |
| Hiring for operations/data | Scaling pressure | Data foundation or reporting package |
| Competitor momentum | Market pressure | Competitive intelligence or roadmap sprint |
| Repeated manual work | Margin leak | Workflow automation assessment |

### 4. Pick the Proposal Shape

Choose one:

- **Diagnostic proposal** — paid assessment, audit, or discovery.
- **Implementation proposal** — clear build scope with milestones.
- **Retainer proposal** — ongoing optimization, monitoring, reporting, and automation.
- **MVP proposal** — short build sprint with focused outcome.

### 5. Draft the Proposal

Use this structure:

1. Situation
2. What the signals show
3. Business risk/opportunity
4. Recommended scope
5. Deliverables
6. Timeline
7. Access/resources needed
8. Pricing model or engagement options
9. Next step

### 6. Add Proof and Boundaries

The proposal must clearly state:

- what evidence was used,
- what is assumed,
- what is out of scope,
- how success will be measured,
- what happens after the first phase.

## Output Template

```markdown
# Proposal: <Client / Opportunity>

## Situation

## What the Signals Show

## Opportunity

## Recommended Scope

## Deliverables

## Timeline

## Required Access and Client Input

## Engagement Options

## Success Criteria

## Next Step
```

## Quality Gates

Before finalizing, verify:

- the proposal is tied to evidence,
- the scope is not too broad,
- the first paid step is easy to approve,
- the value is business-level, not tool-level,
- the next action is clear.
